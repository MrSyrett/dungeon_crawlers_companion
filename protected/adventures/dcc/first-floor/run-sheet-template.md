# Neighborhood → Runnable Adventure: the Run-Sheet Template

A one-page conversion format for turning any *Gamemaster's Campaign Toolkit* Neighborhood or Quest into a session you can actually run. Built for a **2–3 player table playing 2–3 hour sessions**, so one Neighborhood ≈ **two sessions** (cut a quarter to make it one). Every heading below maps onto a tab in the app's **DCC Session Prep Builder** (noted in brackets) so a finished sheet drops straight in.

---

## 0. Header  [Builder → Basics]

| Field | Fill with |
|---|---|
| Title | Neighborhood name + the session's *verb* ("Bogbricks I: Follow the Drums") |
| Floor / Session | Floor number; session number within the floor |
| Players / Duration | "2–3" / "~2.5 hrs" |

## 1. The One-Paragraph Answer  [Builder → Overview]

Answer the book's prep questions **in one paragraph each**, in your own words. If you can't say it in a paragraph you don't understand it well enough to improvise it at the table.

- **What's going on?** — the vibe in one sentence, the mystery in one sentence, the thing the players will *notice first*.
- **Who's the Boss and what does he want?** — the want, then **the weakness the players can find** (the Boss's "Look for Clues" answer). Write the weakness as a *player action*: "If they ___, then ___."
- **How do the Mobs interact?** — which Mob hates which, who can be bribed, who can be pitted against whom. One lever per quarter is plenty.
- **Clock budget** — how many dungeon hours this Neighborhood may cost, and the number at which you start pushing (move NPCs/Mobs into the party's path rather than letting them wander).

## 2. Cold Open  [Builder → Encounters, first card]

Steal one of the write-up's **Strong Starts** and make it the very first thing that happens — *before* anyone asks "what do we see?" Start in media res: something is already taken, biting, or falling. One card: read-aloud text (3–4 sentences, sensory), then the GM note "what the players can do about it right now."

## 3. Scene Ladder — the three phases as cards  [Builder → Encounters]

The book's three player-experience phases become a ladder of **5–8 scene cards**. Each card is one of the builder's three types (**combat / exploration / npc**) and carries a tag line in this shape:

`<Phase> • <Quarter> • <Type> // <Mob group & power level for 2p / 3p>`

**Phase 1 — Opening Discovery (1–2 cards, first quarter).** Show the theme, the sound, the smell. First Mob contact at **Weak** power (party size −1). Plant the first clue about the Boss (in Bogbricks, the drums get faster as you get closer). End the phase at a *fork*: two quarters they could go to next.

**Phase 2 — Mastering the Area (3–4 cards, middle quarters).** A saferoom, an NPC with a want and friction, the neighborhood's signature trap, one **Moderate** fight. This is where the players pick a side, learn the Boss's weakness, and collect the item that trivialises one Boss trick. Put the **session break point** here.

**Phase 3 — Things Blow Up (1–2 cards, last quarter + Boss).** The approach to the lair (a **Moderate** fight or a trap gauntlet — never both, the players need resources for the Boss), then **B-B-B-Boss Battle**. Write the Boss card as: entrance → what the Boss does round 1 → what changes when the players use the weakness → what the allies do if they earned any.

Scene count guide (2–3 players, 2.5 hrs): **cold open + 2 + 3 + 2 = 8 cards over two sessions**, i.e. about four cards a night. Combat cards take ~35–45 min at this table size; exploration/npc cards ~15–20 min.

## 4. Power Levels — sizing fights  [Builder → tag lines]

From the Toolkit's Adversary Power table (Table 3), Mob counts by party size:

| Power | 2 players | 3 players | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak | 1 | 2 | outskirts, first contact, worn-down party |
| Moderate | 2 | 3 | the default fight |
| Strong | 3 | 5 | the fight before the Boss, or when they pit Mobs badly |
| Overwhelming | 4+ | 6+ | run as a trap/chase, not 30 rounds of combat |

Rule of thumb for two sessions: **two Moderate fights per session, one Weak, and the Boss.** Mixing two Mob types counts as a step up in power.

## 5. The Clock  [Builder → Notes]

Floor 1 collapses in **5 days** (150 dungeon hours at 30 hours/day). With 4–6 Neighborhoods on the floor, budget **≈ 24 dungeon hours** per Neighborhood including rest. Write three numbers on the sheet:

- **Comfortable:** the hours they've spent when Session 1 ends (~10).
- **Push:** the number at which you start bringing the Boss's lieutenants *to* them (~18).
- **Hard stop:** when the Neighborhood must be over, Boss or no Boss (~26) — offer the off-ramp (the Boss finds *them*, or the stairwell rumor pulls them out).

Also note which of the Grinding Complications you'll **pre-pick** (don't roll live; choose the one that advances the plot) and which traps can **drain hours** from the clock — those are your accelerator pedal.

## 6. Break Points  [Builder → Notes]

Mark two, so you can end on whichever the table reaches first:

- **The Smaller Goal** (a personal victory): reaching the saferoom, rescuing/allying with the key NPC, disarming the signature trap with flair.
- **The Cliffhanger** (insurmountable obstacle, roll commercials): the Boss's lieutenant demands tribute, the water surfaces something enormous, the lights go out and the gas starts hissing.

## 7. People  [Builder → NPCs/Mobs, type = npc]

One card per named NPC: **Want / Friction / Tell** (one appearance-or-speech trait), plus the *deal* they offer and what happens if the players refuse. If two NPCs oppose each other, write **the fork** explicitly: side with A → these Boss-fight consequences; side with B → those.

## 8. Mobs  [Builder → NPCs/Mobs, type = mob/boss]

Pull the neighborhood's Mobs from the Bestiary picker (or paste the stat lines). Add one line of **tactics in the terrain** to each ("drops from the ceiling", "fights from the water, retreats if hurt") — the numbers are in the book, the *behaviour* is what you'll forget mid-fight.

## 9. Secrets & Clues  [Builder → Clues]

5–7 one-liners the players can uncover, in the order you'd like them found but with no gating. At least two must point at the Boss's weakness; one at the buried/hidden item; one at the NPC conflict.

## 10. Rewards  [Builder → Loot & Rewards]

- The Boss box tier + the map the Boss always drops.
- The write-up's **achievements** as reward cards (the AI reads them aloud — keep the box name, write your own gag if you like).
- The items/skill ranks NPCs can grant, with the condition attached.

## Working method (about an hour)

1. Read the write-up once (20–30 min). Circle the Strong Start you'll use and the one NPC conflict that will carry the Neighborhood.
2. Fill §1 in your own words. If the Boss weakness isn't obvious in the book, invent one from his stat block notes (something he *needs* — drumsticks, silence, his fans).
3. Lay the scene ladder: Cold Open → fork → the middle → the Boss. Assign quarters. Cut one quarter if you only have one night.
4. Size the fights from §4. Write the tag lines.
5. Write the three clock numbers and two break points.
6. Paste NPCs/Mobs, clues, rewards. Done — import into the Session Prep Builder and print.
